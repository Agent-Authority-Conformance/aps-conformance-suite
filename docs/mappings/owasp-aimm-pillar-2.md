# OWASP AIMM Pillar 2 test mapping

This proposed mapping compares the L4 and L5 negative tests in section 5.1 of the OWASP AIMM working draft, as read on 2026-11-18 (the shared document was last modified on 2026-11-11), with published lab cases at [`d5fea20`](https://github.com/Agent-Authority-Conformance/aps-conformance-suite/tree/d5fea20c2949adca9dd70609688ffcd59ebdcd3f). This revision reviewed every candidate directory and fixture family added or changed between the earlier pin `a560b1e` and `d5fea20`: 13 candidate directories and three fixture families. Ten of them add rows below. Five exercise none of the listed failures (aeoess-oauth-actor-profile, aicent-ae128-fail-closed, aicent-pulse-header, aicent-route-shard, action-ref-v1-negatives), and moltycel-aae-vectors updated its evidence record and corrected its description of XP-2's refusal reason, without changing vectors. The test list was reread against the draft on 2026-11-18. The rows and gaps below follow that list. The earlier 2026-10-25 reading was not saved, so what changed between the two readings is not recorded here.

The mapping has no OWASP approval and is not part of AIMM. A match here does not establish that an implementation meets an AIMM level.

## Reading the tables

**Direct** means the case exercises the named failure. **Partial** means it covers only part of the test. Each row states its limits.

- **Conformance** cases target a rule in draft-pidlisnyi-aps-03.
- **Candidate** cases target proposed text. The case sources below name the text and record format.
- **Published candidate** cases are published under `candidates/` through the candidate route, by any submitter including the lab maintainers. They have not been admitted as reviewed fixtures and do not run in lab CI.

Each case links to its source and verification notes. Most use APS records and need an adapter for other formats. A run by the vector author is author-produced evidence, not an independent run. Runtime enforcement needs a test of the running system as well as records.

A verdict of `not_established` or `indeterminate` means the verifier could not establish authority. It is a refusal to accept, not a finding that authority was revoked or refused.

## Delegation rights and unconfirmed completion

**Delegation rights. Partial.** No matching case identified in the inspected lab corpus withdraws a delegator's right to delegate later while its own scope stays usable. The nearest cases test zero remaining delegation depth at one fixed instant. In [conferral without authority][cwa], a delegator holds a valid grant with no remaining hops. A child with exactly the parent's scope (CWA-02), a strictly narrower child (CWA-03), a child at the second hop (CWA-04) and a self-issued child naming the agent itself (CWA-08) are all `invalid` with `DEPTH_EXHAUSTED` (candidate). [Subdelegation edges][sde] `LC-H-008-b` (`DEPTH_EXHAUSTED`, candidate) and [AAE vectors][aae] `aae-vector-09` (`delegation_depth_exceeded`, published candidate) test exhausted depth. [Issuance refusal][irx] `IRX-02-reject-parent-expired` and `IRX-04-reject-parent-revoked` refuse to mint under an expired or revoked parent (conformance). [Argument-designated resources][adr] `ADR-10f` refuses a child from a parent that is not delegable as `authority_issuer_cannot_delegate`, with `ADR-10e` as the delegable control (candidate). This is a fixed state, not a later withdrawal of the right to delegate.

**Unconfirmed completion. Partial.** [Purpose exhaustion][pxe] `PXE-08-reject-unauthenticated-completion` and `PXE-09-reject-completion-from-party-without-standing` reject a completion record with an invalid signature or an attestor without standing. `PXE-10-reject-purchase-after-unauthenticated-completion` and `PXE-11-reject-purchase-after-completion-without-standing` refuse another purchase under the same purpose-bound grant as `bound_state_not_established`. Refusal in this uncertain exhaustion state is the fixture's chosen policy. These are candidates against the authority lifecycle text, using APS grant and receipt records plus a fixture-defined purpose bound. They do not test an action requiring successful completion of an earlier operation. Rejecting a completion record does not show that no effect happened.

## Remaining gaps

Each line names a test with no matching case identified in the inspected lab corpus, and says what the nearest rows lack.

- Critical operation without both required approvals (L4). LC-C-011 tests the two-role gate on records and never attempts the operation or checks that it does not run.
- Just-in-time grant (L4). A fresh grant required for one critical call, with the call refused without it.
- Concurrent single-use consumption (L4). ASU-07 runs its two calls one after the other on one instance.
- Concurrent shared-limit accounting (L4). Two distinct valid approvals drawing on one shared budget at the same time. ASU-07 and the denial continuity cases use no shared budget.
- Crash and restart recovery against single-use or shared limits (L4). ASE-07 takes cache loss as an input and never restarts a boundary after a permit or budget was used, then retries.
- Descendant use after parent revocation beyond the propagation limit, in one case (L4). CAR checks supplied call times beyond the propagation limit but models no chain. AAC and LC-H-009 check chains without a maximum propagation delay. Relationship revocation propagation observes inherited permission after an upstream relationship is removed, against declared thresholds that include the submitter's reading of cache settings, but it tests relationship dependencies rather than delegated agent grants and invokes no protected operation.
- A valid grant from another domain refused by the receiving domain's own policy (L5). PC-11 and ARAP A7 refuse inside one domain, and A7's reason is a disabled subject.
- Use of a derived grant after the delegator's right to delegate is removed, beyond the propagation limit (L4), while the delegator's own scope stays usable. CWA tests zero remaining depth at one instant, and ADR-10f a parent that was never delegable.
- Dependent derived authority used after its upstream authority stops covering the operation, beyond the propagation limit (L4). AEX-04 and AEX-05 test an upstream expiry that takes effect at once.
- Retries after an unknown outcome (L4). PXE-10 and PXE-11 block a subsequent purchase, but do not track repeated attempts of the same operation or check when its authority can safely be released.
- Resource effects at the declared commitment point (L4). Running call halt compares supplied receipts and observer values. It does not determine actual resource changes or test limit changes, failure or recovery at that point.
- A copy of the runtime unable to produce a valid proof for a non-exportable bound key (L4). TQB-03 refuses an unregistered attestation key but asks for no proof of possession of the workload key.

Anyone can contribute a candidate for these gaps under [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md), using their own record format and terms.
